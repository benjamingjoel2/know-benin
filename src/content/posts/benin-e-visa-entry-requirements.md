The confirmation email arrives while you are still deciding what to pack: a request number, then, a little later, the visa itself. It is a small thing, a document on a screen, but it is the one thing that stands between you and the warm, diesel-scented air of Cotonou when the aircraft door opens. Get it right and arrival is a queue and a stamp. Get it wrong and there is very little anyone at the airport can do for you.

This guide sets out how Benin's e-visa works, what the official sources say about passports and vaccinations, and where those sources do not quite agree. Rules change, so treat everything here as correct at the time of writing (November 2026) and check the official pages linked below before you book.

## Do you need a visa?

Most visitors do. The UK government's [Benin entry requirements page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) is blunt about it for British passport holders: "You must have a visa to enter or travel through Benin." [France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/information-par-pays/benin/conseils-aux-voyageurs-entree-sejour) says the same for French nationals. Note the word "through": on the FCDO's wording, you need a visa even if you are only passing through.

The exception set out on Benin's own e-visa portal concerns African nationals. The portal's [frequently asked questions](https://evisa.bj/foire-aux-questions) state that "les Africains peuvent visiter le Bénin sans visa pendant 90 jours": Africans can visit Benin without a visa for 90 days. The portal also has a page on countries with which Benin has reciprocal visa-exemption agreements, but at the time of writing [that page](https://evisa.bj/articles/voyager-sans-visa-pays-exemptes/96a23d9e-1846-468d-9d55-1af98358464e) does not list the countries itself; it refers readers to Benin's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. If you think you may be exempt, confirm it with a Beninese embassy before you travel rather than relying on a third-party list.

For everyone else, the answer is the e-visa.

## The three e-visa types

Benin's official portal is [evisa.bj](https://evisa.bj/) (the older address, evisa.gouv.bj, now redirects there). It offers three visas, according to the portal's [fee page](https://evisa.bj/articles/les-couts-des-visas-dentree-au-benin/8c948600-ca08-443e-bac9-33aae6583b54):

- **30 days, single entry**
- **30 days, multiple entry**
- **90 days, multiple entry**

The portal's FAQ advises simply that you choose the visa according to the length of your stay. The validity runs from the entry date you give in the "Données du voyage" (travel details) section of the form, so enter the date you actually expect to land, not the date you happen to apply.

If you plan to slip across into Togo and come back, the multiple-entry options matter. We cover that kind of route in [Combining Benin with Togo and Ghana](/stories/benin-togo-ghana/). And bear in mind the FCDO's warning that you cannot get visas for Nigeria, Ghana or Togo while you are in Benin: apply for those before you leave home.

## What it costs

At the time of writing, the fee page on the official portal lists these prices for applying online yourself:

- 30 days, single entry: 50 euros
- 30 days, multiple entry: 75 euros
- 90 days, multiple entry: 100 euros

The same page lists higher prices (60, 85 and 110 euros respectively) when the application goes through an embassy, which matches France Diplomatie's note that travellers can still apply at a Beninese consulate "moyennant le paiement de frais supplémentaires", for an additional fee.

One caution: that fee page is dated December 2020. It is still the page the official portal links to, but the amount you are shown at the payment step is the one that counts.

Payment is by card only. The portal's [how-it-works page](https://evisa.bj/howitworks) says it accepts Visa and Mastercard with 3D Secure, and the FAQ adds that the payment platform only supports cards with 3DS 2. If your card is older or your bank blocks overseas online payments, sort that out first.

## How to apply, step by step

The process on the [official portal](https://evisa.bj/howitworks) runs like this:

1. **Start the request.** On the home page, click "Demander un e-Visa", enter and confirm your email address, and give your passport number and its expiry date. You then confirm the request through a link sent to your email.
2. **Fill in the form.** The link opens a form in four parts: personal details, e-visa details, travel details and passport details. Fields marked with an asterisk are required, and the portal stresses that what you enter must match your identity and travel documents.
3. **Pay.** "Confirmer et payer" takes you to the payment platform.
4. **Track it.** Once payment is confirmed, you receive an email with a request number, which you can use with "Suivre votre demande" on the home page.

The FCDO says the e-visa itself is sent by email, and France Diplomatie describes it arriving as a barcode. We'd print a copy and keep a digital one on your phone as well.

Read the form twice before you pay. The FAQ is unambiguous on two points: an e-visa cannot be changed once it has been issued, because issuing is automated, and fees are not refunded. A mistyped passport number is your problem to fix, at your expense.

## How long it takes, and when to apply

The official sources give slightly different timings, so plan around the most cautious:

- The portal's [FAQ](https://evisa.bj/foire-aux-questions) says processing can take from one hour to 96 hours, recommends applying at least 7 days before you enter Benin, and says you cannot apply more than 90 days before your entry date.
- The [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) tells British travellers to apply at least 4 working days before arrival.

We'd apply once your flights are fixed and you are inside that 90-day window, and certainly not in the final week.

## Passport validity

Here the official sources differ, and the stricter one is the safer guide.

- The [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) says your passport "must be valid for the duration of your stay", and that you will be refused entry with a passport that has been reported lost or stolen.
- [France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/information-par-pays/benin/conseils-aux-voyageurs-entree-sejour) says French nationals need a passport valid for at least six months after their return date, plus a return ticket.

Your passport's expiry date goes into the application, so if it is close to expiring, renew it before you apply.

## Yellow fever: a change worth knowing about

This is the area where official information is currently out of step, so it is worth reading slowly.

On 16 September 2026 the e-visa portal published a [notice from Benin's Ministry of Health](https://evisa.bj/articles/controle-du-carnet-de-vaccination-fievre-jaune-aux-points-dentree-du-benin/0225cd59-9365-4534-94ee-c1e3cdc996c7) announcing that vaccination records, "notamment celui de la fièvre jaune", are no longer checked at entry points (airport, port and land borders). The same notice says Benin reserves the right to reverse this at any time in an international health emergency, and that yellow fever vaccination remains recommended for all travellers.

At the time of writing, other official sources still describe a certificate as a condition of entry:

- The [FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements): "To enter Benin, you must have a certificate to prove you've had a yellow fever vaccination."
- [France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/information-par-pays/benin/conseils-aux-voyageurs-sante) says vaccination is obligatory to enter Benin and should be done at an approved centre before departure.
- [TravelHealthPro](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/27/benin) says proof of vaccination is required for all travellers aged 9 months or over, and that there is a risk of yellow fever transmission throughout the country.
- The [US CDC](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/benin) lists the vaccine as required for arriving travellers aged 9 months or over, and recommended for all travellers of that age.

Our advice is simple: get vaccinated and carry the certificate. The disease risk is real whatever happens at the border, the check could be reinstated without notice, and neighbouring countries have their own rules. TravelHealthPro notes that under World Health Organization rules a yellow fever certificate is now valid for life. The vaccine is not suitable for everyone, so talk to a travel health professional well before you go. For malaria and the rest of the health picture, see [The first-timer's guide to Benin](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-benin/).

## Arriving in Cotonou

If you are flying in to Cotonou, have your passport, the printed e-visa and your yellow fever certificate together in your hand luggage, along with the address of your first night's accommodation.

A few official points to keep in mind on arrival:

- **No cameras.** The FCDO's [safety page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security) says it is illegal to take photos at or near sensitive sites "such as military installations or the airport". Keep your phone away until you are well clear.
- **Customs.** The FCDO says there are strict rules on what you can bring in or take out, and you must declare anything that may be prohibited or subject to tax or duty. France Diplomatie adds that medicines for personal use, in reasonable quantities, are not restricted.
- **"Gifts".** The FCDO advises politely and firmly declining any request from officials for a "gift" to smooth administrative matters.

If you are arriving late, we'd arrange a pick-up in advance rather than negotiating at the kerb. The FCDO advises avoiding driving after dark, and France Diplomatie advises against motorbike taxis.

## If your plans change

If your e-visa runs out while you are still in Benin, the portal's FAQ says to contact the [Direction de l'Emigration et de l'Immigration](https://dei.gouv.bj/) to extend it. Do this before the expiry date, not after.

## What is unclear, and what to check

At the time of writing (November 2026), these points are worth checking against official sources close to your departure:

- **Yellow fever.** Benin's Ministry of Health says certificates are no longer checked at entry; the FCDO, France Diplomatie, TravelHealthPro and the CDC still describe one as required. Carry it.
- **Fees.** The portal's fee page dates from 2020. The price at the payment step is the one that applies.
- **Exemptions.** The portal says Africans can visit visa-free for 90 days, but the reciprocal-exemption list itself sits with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, not on the portal.
- **Passport validity.** The UK and French governments word this differently. If in doubt, allow at least six months.
- **Your own government's advice.** We could not load the US State Department's Benin pages when checking this post. American travellers should read [travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/) directly, and everyone should check their own government's travel advice before booking.
