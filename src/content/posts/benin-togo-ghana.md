The sun drops into the Gulf of Guinea and the beach at Lomé fills with people: families walking at the edge of the surf, footballers on the hard sand, someone selling grilled corn from a smoking brazier. Behind you is a capital city; a short drive east, past the lagoons, is another country. On this stretch of West African coast, borders come quickly.

That is what makes a combined trip so tempting. Accra, Lomé and Cotonou sit on the same shoreline, and the story that links them, the Atlantic slave trade, runs from the castles of Ghana's Central Region to the Door of No Return at Ouidah. Here is how to put the three countries together, and what to sort out before you go.

## The coastal route at a glance

Most combined trips follow the coast in one direction or the other. Running west to east, the classic line is:

1. **Accra**, Ghana's capital and the usual international arrival point.
2. **Cape Coast and Elmina**, for the castles of the Gold Coast.
3. **Lomé**, Togo's capital, with side trips to Lake Togo and the hills around Kpalimé.
4. **Grand-Popo**, the first stop on the Benin side, among beaches and lagoons.
5. **Ouidah**, for the Route des Esclaves and Vodun heritage.
6. **Cotonou**, for Ganvié, Porto-Novo and your flight home.

You can just as easily run it the other way. We'd fly into one end and out of the other, rather than doubling back, and build in a spare day at each border: crossings can be slow, and the FCDO notes that [Togo's borders can close at short notice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/togo/safety-and-security).

## Travel advice for each country

All three countries have parts of their north where the UK advises against travel. The coastal route avoids those areas, but it is worth knowing where they are. At the time of writing (November 2026):

- **Ghana.** The FCDO [advises against all but essential travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/warnings-and-insurance) to the Bawku Municipal area in the Upper East region. Its [safety page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/safety-and-security) notes that street crime such as pickpocketing and bag-snatching is on the increase in Accra.
- **Togo.** The FCDO [advises against all travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/togo) to within 30km of the border with Burkina Faso (except the city of Dapaong and the N1 highway leading to it from the south), and against all but essential travel to the rest of the Savanes region. It says [violent crime, theft and pickpocketing are common](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/togo/safety-and-security), particularly on the beaches and in the markets of Lomé, and advises against walking alone, especially at night.
- **Benin.** The FCDO [advises against all travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/warnings-and-insurance) to Pendjari and W national parks, their hunting zones and areas within 5km of the Burkina Faso border, and against all but essential travel to the rest of the Alibori and Atacora departments, among other areas. The south, where this route runs, is not covered by those warnings.

The FCDO says terrorist attacks are very likely in Togo, warns of a risk of attacks in Benin's northern border regions, and says attacks cannot be ruled out in Ghana. Check your own government's advice for every country on your route, and remember that [insurance can be invalidated](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/warnings-and-insurance) if you travel against it. That includes side trips: Togo's Koutammakou or Benin's [Tata Somba country](/stories/tata-somba-houses-atakora/) are a long way north of the coast.

### Do not drive after dark

This is the one rule we'd underline for a road trip. The FCDO tells travellers in Benin to [avoid driving outside towns and cities at night](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security), and in Ghana to [avoid road travel outside the main towns after dark](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/safety-and-security). In Togo it warns that [roads outside towns are poorly lit](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/togo/safety-and-security) and that official and unofficial roadblocks occur. Plan each day so you reach the next town, and ideally cross each border, in daylight.

### The sea

It is a beautiful coast and a dangerous one to swim. The FCDO warns of rip tides and undertows on Ghana's southern beaches, very strong currents and drownings each year along Togo's coast, and very strong currents along Benin's coast, including at Ouidah. Paddle, don't swim, unless locals tell you a spot is safe.

## Visas: one for each country

Do not assume one visa covers the region. For British passport holders, at the time of writing:

- **Ghana.** The FCDO says you [must have a visa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/entry-requirements) and need to apply in advance, and that your passport must be valid for at least six months from the date you arrive. The Ghana Immigration Service runs an [official eVisa portal](https://evisa.immigration.gov.gh/visa-information) with an eligibility checker by nationality; check it alongside the FCDO page, as the two describe the process differently.
- **Togo.** You [must have a visa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/togo/entry-requirements), and you cannot get one on arrival. The FCDO says to apply for an e-visa on the [official portal](https://voyage.gouv.tg/) at least five working days before arrival; a single-entry tourist visa lasts 15 days. Your passport must be valid for at least three months after you plan to leave Togo.
- **Benin.** You [must have a visa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements), applied for online on the [official e-visa platform](https://evisa.bj/) at least four working days before you arrive. Your passport must be valid for the length of your stay. Our [e-visa guide](/stories/benin-e-visa-entry-requirements/) goes through it step by step.

One catch for this route in particular: the FCDO says you [cannot get visas for Ghana or Togo inside Benin](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements), so arrange every visa before you leave home. Make sure each one's dates fit your plan, especially Togo's 15 days.

### ECOWAS citizens

If you hold a passport from an ECOWAS member state, the rules are different. Under the [ECOWAS free movement protocol](https://ecowas.int/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PROTOCOL-RELATING-TO-FREE-MOVEMENT-OF-PERSONS.pdf), community citizens can enter another member state through an official entry point without a visa for stays of up to 90 days, with a valid travel document and an international health certificate. Togo's portal also says citizens of African countries can stay up to 30 days without a visa if they complete the online immigration form at least 24 hours before arrival. This does not apply to non-ECOWAS passports, whatever your country of residence.

## Health: yellow fever and more

All three countries require proof of yellow fever vaccination. TravelHealthPro says a certificate is required for travellers aged nine months or over entering [Benin](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/27/benin), [Togo](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/222/togo) and [Ghana](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/89/ghana), and the FCDO entry pages for each country say the same. Carry the original certificate with your passport for every crossing. Ghana also asks for it with your visa application.

At the time of writing, the FCDO says there is [additional Ebola screening at Ghana's border entry points](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/entry-requirements) and that travellers should complete the online Ghana Health Declaration before travelling. TravelHealthPro rates the risk of malaria in Benin as high. See a travel health clinic well before you go.

## The border crossings

There are two land borders on the coastal route, both on the main coast road:

- **Ghana–Togo: Aflao and Kodjoviakopé.** Aflao is the Ghanaian side, Kodjoviakopé the Togolese side. The crossing is named this way in an [IOM study](https://rodakar.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl696/files/documents/2023-03/Study%20on%20Immigration-Public%20Health%20and%20Trade%20Facilitation-EN.pdf) of the region's border posts.
- **Togo–Benin: Sanvee Condji and Hillacondji.** Sanvee Condji is in Togo; Hillacondji (spelled Hilla-Condji by [Benin's customs service](https://douanes.gouv.bj/en/professional_service/dedouanement-a-la-frontiere-dhilla-condji/)) is in Benin.

At each, you leave one country's immigration and customs before entering the next. Have printed copies of your visas, your yellow fever certificate and your onward plans to hand. If you are on a tour, ask your operator exactly how the hand-over at each border works.

## The heritage thread: castles and the Door of No Return

![The seaward battery of Cape Coast Castle, Ghana, with cannons facing the sea](photo:cape-coast)

The strongest reason to combine these countries is the history they share. On Ghana's coast, UNESCO lists the [Forts and Castles, Volta, Greater Accra, Central and Western Regions](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/34/), inscribed in 1979: a chain of fortified trading posts founded between 1482 and 1786. It includes three castles, Cape Coast, St George's d'Elmina and Christiansborg at Osu in Accra, and 15 forts. UNESCO describes them as a symbol of the slave trade and of nearly four centuries of pre-colonial Afro-European commerce, and as a starting point of the African diaspora. St George's at Elmina, built in 1482, is one of the oldest European buildings outside Europe.

Follow the coast east and the same story reaches Ouidah, where a memorial route leads from the old town to the beach. Our guide to [Ouidah and the Route des Esclaves](/stories/ouidah-route-des-esclaves/) walks the memorial route to the Door of No Return. Seeing the castles and then Ouidah within a week or two gives the history a scale that one site alone cannot.

## Togo's stops

Togo is often treated as a corridor between Ghana and Benin, but it rewards a few days:

- **Lomé.** Togo's capital has a long beach along its seafront, busy at sunset. Keep your guard up there in the evening; this is where the FCDO's crime warning is focused.
- **Lake Togo and Togoville.** About 30km east of Lomé, the lake is crossed by pirogue to Togoville, which [Le Routard](https://www.routard.com/guide_voyage_lieu/4495-lac_togo.htm) describes as an important spiritual centre with Vodun convents and shrines. As in Benin, ask before you photograph.
- **Kpalimé.** About 120km from Lomé in the forested hills of the Plateaux region, Kpalimé is a base for guided walks, waterfalls such as Kpimé, and Mont Agou, at 986m Togo's highest point, according to [Le Routard](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/afrique/togo/region-des-plateaux). It is also known for its craft centre.

All three are in southern Togo, outside the areas the FCDO names in its warnings at the time of writing. The FCDO's general advice for Togo still applies.

## The Benin leg

![A single palm tree on a wide, empty sandy beach at Grand-Popo](photo:grand-popo)

After crossing at Hillacondji, Grand-Popo makes a good first night in Benin among the beaches and lagoons of the far south-west. From there it is a short hop to Ouidah, then on to Cotonou. From Cotonou you can reach [Ganvié](/stories/ganvie-village-on-stilts/), Porto-Novo and, as a day trip or overnight, the [royal palaces of Abomey](/stories/royal-palaces-of-abomey/). If your dates fall in early January, consider building the trip around [Vodun Days in Ouidah](/stories/vodun-days-ouidah/). Our [10-day itinerary](/stories/benin-10-day-itinerary/) has more ideas for the Benin end, and the [first-timer's guide](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-benin/) covers money, transport and etiquette.

## Before you book

A three-country trip has more moving parts than a single-country one: three visas with different lead times, two land borders, and three sets of travel advice to keep an eye on. A local operator in each country, or one who works with trusted partners across the borders, can take much of that weight off you, from meeting you at the frontier to adjusting the plan if the advice changes.
