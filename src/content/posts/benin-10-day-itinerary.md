Morning in Porto-Novo. The street outside the Great Mosque is already busy, and its facade, more like a Brazilian church than anything you expected to see in West Africa, glows in the low sun. In ten days you will have crossed a lake to a town on stilts, walked the road enslaved people walked to the sea at Ouidah, watched a river meet the Atlantic at Grand-Popo, and stood in the red-earth courtyards of the kings of Abomey. None of it is far apart. Southern Benin is compact, and that is its great gift to a first-time visitor.

This is our suggested route for ten days in the south and centre of the country. It is built to be done at an unhurried pace, by car with a driver, and always in daylight.

## Before you start: the advice that shapes this route

### Why the north is not in this itinerary

Northern Benin is home to two of the country's great sights, Pendjari National Park and the Tata Somba houses of the Atakora hills. They are not in this itinerary, for one reason: official advice.

At the time of writing (November 2026), the [UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO)](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin) advises against all travel to Pendjari National Park and its adjacent hunting grounds, to Parc du W and the Mékrou and Djona hunting zones, to the area between Parc du W and the Niger border, and to all other areas within 5 km of the Burkina Faso border. It advises against all but essential travel to the rest of the Alibori and Atacora departments, which include Natitingou and the Tata Somba country, and to the area between the RNIE 2 highway (Tchaourou to Malanville) and the Nigerian border. The FCDO also says there is a [heightened risk of kidnap](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security) in areas bordering the Sahel, including northern Benin.

Travelling against your government's advice can also invalidate your travel insurance. Check your own government's advice, not only the UK's, before you book.

If the advice changes, the north could be added as a second week. Read our posts on [safari in Pendjari National Park](/stories/pendjari-national-park-safari/) and [the Tata Somba houses of the Atakora](/stories/tata-somba-houses-atakora/) to see what that might look like, and check the advice again before you plan it.

### No driving after dark

The FCDO advises [avoiding driving outside towns and cities at night](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security), as roads are poorly lit, and notes that driving standards and road conditions are poor. Every day in this route is planned so you arrive before dusk. If a day's sightseeing runs late, stay where you are rather than pushing on.

### Paperwork and health

British passport holders need a visa, applied for online [at least four working days before arrival](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) through the official [Benin e-visa portal](https://evisa.bj/). The FCDO says you must have a [yellow fever vaccination certificate](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) to enter; Benin's Ministry of Health said in September 2026 that certificates are no longer checked at entry points, but the vaccine is still recommended, so get vaccinated and carry the certificate. Our guide to [Benin's e-visa and entry](/stories/benin-e-visa-entry-requirements/) explains the process, and our [first-timer's guide](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-benin/) covers the rest of the basics.

## Day 1: Arrive in Cotonou

Cotonou is where you land and where you will finish, so treat the first day as a soft start. Settle in, change some money, get a local SIM, and rest. The FCDO notes that [street crime, including theft and mugging, is common in Cotonou](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security), so keep valuables out of sight and use a car arranged through your hotel or operator after dark.

## Day 2: Porto-Novo

Porto-Novo is Benin's capital, about 30 km from Cotonou according to the [Government of Benin](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1048/destination-benin---porto-novo--condense-histoire-culture-fete-adoucit-moeurs/). It became the capital of the French colony of Dahomey and kept the role after independence in 1960; the National Assembly sits here.

Spend the day on foot:

- the **Great Mosque**, with its Afro-Brazilian architecture;
- the **Honmè museum** and the **palace of King Toffa I**;
- the **Alexandre Sènou Adandé ethnographic museum** and the **da Silva museum** of Afro-Brazilian arts and culture.

The government also points to the city's Zangbeto and Egungun traditions. If you see a masquerade, watch from a respectful distance and ask before photographing.

## Day 3: Ganvié at dawn

Leave early for the jetty at Abomey-Calavi, on the north side of Cotonou. The government describes the [crossing to Ganvié](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1587/mise-tourisme-rapide-embarcadere-abomey-calavi-descente-ministre-abimbola-station-touristique/) as about fifteen minutes on the water. Ganvié, a town on stilts in Lake Nokoué, was founded by people escaping slave raids, and it is still home to a fishing community. Go with a guide, buy tickets at the official counter, and ask before photographing anyone. Our post on [Ganvié, the village on stilts](/stories/ganvie-village-on-stilts/) covers the history and how a visit works.

You will be back on dry land by late morning. Spend the afternoon in Cotonou, or rest.

## Days 4 and 5: Ouidah

Ouidah is about 30 km west of Cotonou, says the [Government of Benin](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1055/), a short drive along the coast. Give it two days; it deserves time to absorb.

On the first day, walk the **Route de l'Esclave**, the 3 km memorial route from the auction square to the Door of No Return on the beach. It passes the Tree of Forgetting, the Zomaï enclosure where captives were held in darkness, the Zoungbodji memorial and the Memorial of Repentance. Walk it slowly, and with a guide if you can.

![Visitors on the red-sand esplanade walking toward the Door of No Return arch, Ouidah](photo:door-of-no-return)

On the second day, see the **Ouidah History Museum** in the old Portuguese fort, and the square where the **Temple of Pythons** faces the Catholic basilica, which the government cites as an example of religions living side by side. Ouidah is an important centre of Vodun, a living religion; ask before photographing shrines or ceremonies. Our guide to [Ouidah and the Route des Esclaves](/stories/ouidah-route-des-esclaves/) goes deeper.

If your dates include 10 January, the national day of endogenous religions, read our post on [the Vodun Days festival in Ouidah](/stories/vodun-days-ouidah/) and book accommodation early.

## Day 6: Grand-Popo and the Bouche du Roy

Continue west along the coast to Grand-Popo, a beach town between the Mono river and the Atlantic, close to the Togo border. The [Government of Benin](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1012/destination-benin---grand-popo--cite-balneaire-bercee-ocean-atlantique-fleuve-mono/) highlights pirogue excursions on the Mono and motorboat trips to the Bouche du Roy, where the river reaches the sea. It also notes the ruins of colonial trading posts at Gbécon, and that coastal erosion since the 1950s has swallowed much of the old town.

A word on the sea. The FCDO warns that [ocean currents are very strong along all parts of the coast and many drownings occur each year](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security). Enjoy the beach; think hard before swimming, and ask locally.

![A single palm on a wide sandy beach at Grand-Popo](photo:grand-popo)

## Day 7: Lake Ahémé and Possotomè

Turn inland to Lake Ahémé. The Government of Benin's page on [Bopa](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1002/destination-benin---bopa-cite-bleu/), the commune on its shore, says the lake is known for pirogue trips, with places to relax along its banks. The thermal spring at Possotomè feeds a mineral water bottled for the whole country. Fishing is the daily life of the lake, so this is a good day to slow down: a boat trip, a lakeside lunch of local fish, an early night.

## Day 8: To Abomey

This is the longest drive of the trip. Abomey is about 145 km from Cotonou, according to the [Government of Benin](https://www.gouv.bj/article/1018/destination-benin---abomey-intraitable-cite-conquerants-%22l-empire-romain%22-golfe-guinee/), and from the lake it is a long drive inland. Leave after breakfast so you arrive with daylight to spare, and spend a quiet evening in town.

## Day 9: The royal palaces of Abomey

Abomey was the capital of the kingdom of Danxomè, and its royal palaces are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Spend the day on the palaces and their painted bas-reliefs with a local guide. A new museum and a major restoration of five palaces have been under way, and at the time of writing we could not confirm what is open, so ask your operator before you go. Our post on [the royal palaces of Abomey](/stories/royal-palaces-of-abomey/) has the history, the story of the 2021 return of 26 royal treasures from France, and the questions to ask.

## Day 10: Back to Cotonou

Drive back south in the morning, with time for last purchases in Cotonou before an evening flight. If your flight leaves early, swap days and spend the last night in Cotonou instead.

### Optional: Dassa-Zoumé

If you have an eleventh day, continue from Abomey to Dassa-Zoumé in the Collines department, about 209 km from Cotonou according to the [Government of Benin](https://www.gouv.bj/article/983/). It is known for its 41 hills and for the Marian grotto of Arigbo, dedicated in 1954, which draws Catholic pilgrims every year. It sits in the centre of the country, outside the areas covered by the FCDO's warnings at the time of writing, but check the latest advice before you go, and still plan the drive back to Cotonou in daylight.

## Making it work

- **Travel with a driver.** It saves you from the road conditions and frees you to look out of the window.
- **Build in slack.** We'd add a spare day somewhere in the middle, for heat, rain or a ceremony you were invited to.
- **Season.** Escale Voyages, for example, runs its longer group journeys between November and March; ask your operator about weather and festivals for your dates.
- **Reverse it if you like.** The route works the other way round, starting in Abomey.
- **Combining with neighbours.** If you want to continue west into Togo and Ghana, see our post on [combining Benin with Togo and Ghana](/stories/benin-togo-ghana/).

## Planning with a local operator

Ten days is enough to see the heart of southern Benin without rushing it. A Cotonou-based operator can arrange drivers, guides and the boat at Ganvié, keep an eye on the latest advice, and tell you what is open in Abomey this month. Ask them how they plan their days around the no-driving-after-dark rule, and whether any part of their wider journeys goes into areas covered by FCDO warnings.
