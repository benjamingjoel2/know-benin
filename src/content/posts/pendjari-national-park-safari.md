The light comes up grey, then gold, over a flat land of grass and borassus palms. A line of elephants moves between the trunks without hurry, ears lifting, dust rising behind them. Somewhere off to the left a waterhole catches the sun. This is Pendjari, in the far north-west of Benin, and it is one of the few places left in West Africa where a morning can look like this.

It is also, at the time of writing, a place the UK government tells its citizens not to go. This post explains both halves of that: why Pendjari matters, how a visit has worked in calmer times, and what to do with your trip instead while the advice stands.

## The travel advice, plainly

At the time of writing (November 2026), the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) [advises against all travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/warnings-and-insurance) to Pendjari National Park and its adjacent hunting grounds, to Parc du W and the Mékrou and Djona hunting zones, to the area between Parc du W and the Niger border, and to all other areas within 5km of the Burkina Faso border. It advises against all but essential travel to the rest of the Alibori and Atacora departments, which takes in Natitingou and Tanguiéta, the towns on the road to the park. The FCDO page was last updated on 4 November 2026.

The FCDO's [safety and security page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security) says there is a heightened risk of kidnap in areas bordering the Sahel, including northern Benin, and a risk of terrorist attacks in the northern border regions.

Other governments say much the same. [France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/conseils-aux-voyageurs/conseils-par-pays-destination/benin/) classes the northern border zones, including the Pendjari and W parks and the areas around them, as "formellement déconseillé" (red), and says this level is incompatible with tourism (page updated 16 September 2026). The [US State Department](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/benin.html) tells US citizens not to travel to Benin's northern border region and restricts US government employees from personal travel to Pendjari and W parks.

Two practical points follow. First, check your own government's advice, not ours, and check it again just before you travel. Second, the FCDO warns that [your travel insurance could be invalidated](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/warnings-and-insurance) if you travel against its advice. We are not going to suggest ways round that.

## The park and the W-Arly-Pendjari complex

Pendjari is not an island. It is one piece of a much larger block of protected land that straddles Benin, Burkina Faso and Niger. In 2017, UNESCO's World Heritage Committee ([decision 41 COM 8B.3](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6867/)) approved extending Niger's W National Park, listed in 1996, to become the [W-Arly-Pendjari Complex](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/749/), inscribed for its natural values under criteria (ix) and (x).

The complex brings together the trinational W Regional Park, Arly National Park in Burkina Faso, Pendjari National Park in Benin, and several hunting zones, including Konkombri and Mékrou on the Benin side. Taken together it is the kind of landscape that has largely vanished elsewhere in the region: savannah, gallery forest along the rivers, and seasonal waterholes where animals gather.

## African Parks and the park today

On 31 May 2017, the Presidency of Benin and the conservation organisation African Parks announced a [10-year renewable partnership](https://www.africanparks.org/major-agreement-revitalise-last-large-wildlife-reserve-west-and-central-africa) to rehabilitate and manage Pendjari. African Parks described it as one of the largest remaining protected reserves in West and Central Africa. The plans set out at the time included a perimeter fence, new roads, guard posts and ranger bases, and an aim to grow visitor numbers.

African Parks has since extended its work to Benin's part of W National Park, and in 2024 it reported fitting [satellite collars](https://www.africanparks.org/largest-collaring-initiative-concluded-protect-wildlife-pendjari-and-w-national-parks-benin) to elephants and antelopes across both parks to track their movements.

## The 2019 kidnapping and what came after

In 2019, two French tourists and their Beninese guide were kidnapped in Pendjari. The FCDO's [safety and security page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/safety-and-security) records that the guide was later found dead and the hostages were freed in Burkina Faso days later. African Parks' [2019 review](https://www.africanparks.org/2019-year-review) notes the park closed to tourism after the incident and reopened in October that year.

The security picture across the wider complex has worsened since. In February 2022, attacks with improvised explosive devices in W National Park killed rangers, drivers, an instructor and a soldier, according to [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2420). The FCDO lists further deadly attacks on military posts in Parc du W in January and April 2025. These attacks were in W rather than Pendjari, but they are part of why advice for the whole northern border belt is where it is.

We mention this not to dramatise it but because anyone reading older trip reports from Pendjari should know the context has changed.

## The wildlife

![An elephant standing at the muddy edge of the Mare Sacrée waterhole in Pendjari](photo:pendjari-mare)

African Parks lists [elephant, buffalo, West African lion, cheetah and leopard](https://www.africanparks.org/the-parks/pendjari) among the park's large mammals, along with hippo, tsessebe, hartebeest, Buffon's kob and defassa waterbuck, and more than 460 bird species.

Two of those deserve a closer look:

- **Elephants.** African Parks describes Pendjari's elephants as the largest population in the region, within a W-Arly-Pendjari complex that holds most of West Africa's remaining elephants.
- **West African lions.** These are critically endangered. African Parks describes Pendjari as a stronghold for them and says the W-Arly-Pendjari complex is part of a small group of protected areas where most of the remaining population survives.

Pendjari has never been a "big five on tap" park in the East African mould. The appeal is a wild, quiet savannah where seeing a lion is a real event, not a traffic jam.

## When to go, if advice changes

African Parks says the park is [open to visitors throughout the year](https://www.africanparks.org/the-parks/pendjari/visit-pendjari-0), even in the rainy season, but warns that from August to October heavy rain can leave water standing on the roads. It also notes that animals assemble around seasonal and permanent water sources, That points to the dry months, when water is scarce and wildlife is drawn to the remaining pools, though African Parks does not name a best season. Escale Voyages, for example, schedules its northern journey between December and April.

## How a visit has worked, when advice allows

None of what follows is a suggestion to go now. It is how Pendjari trips have been put together, so you can judge an operator's plan if and when official advice changes.

![A tarmac road cutting through the rocky Atakora hills near Tanguiéta](photo:atakora-road)

**The road north.** From Cotonou it is a long drive. Escale Voyages' itinerary breaks it with a night at Abomey, then a full day through Savalou and Djougou to Natitingou, the main town of the Atakora. Tanguiéta, further on, is the park's postal base ([BP 32, Tanguiéta](https://www.africanparks.org/the-parks/pendjari/contact)) and the last town before the gates.

**Guides and vehicles.** Visits have run as guided game drives, at dawn and late afternoon, often in park vehicles. Benin's government registered tourism guides in October 2026 under a new decree, according to our sister site [Trusted Ground](https://trustedground.tourground.com/articles/benin-abomey-ouidah/). If you book a guide, ask whether they are registered.

**Park entry and nights inside.** Entry fees and permits are paid to the park. Escale's itinerary, for example, uses a riverside lodge as a base for three nights, so you can be out at first light. We'd build in two or three nights at least; the drive in is too long for less.

**Questions to ask any operator, before advice changes or after:**

- Which official advice are you following, and on what date did you last check it?
- What happens to my trip, and my money, if the advice changes before or during travel?
- Who are your guides, and are they registered?
- Does my insurance cover every place on this itinerary?

## What to do instead, now

The good news is that most of what draws people to Benin is in the south, where the FCDO's area-specific warnings do not apply. The FCDO's general advice for the country, including on crime and night driving, still does.

- **Ganvié.** Take a pirogue across Lake Nokoué to the village on stilts. Our guide to [Ganvié](/stories/ganvie-village-on-stilts/) covers how to go respectfully.
- **Ouidah.** Walk the [Route des Esclaves](/stories/ouidah-route-des-esclaves/) to the Door of No Return, and, if your dates fit, plan around [Vodun Days](/stories/vodun-days-ouidah/) in January.
- **Abomey.** The [royal palaces](/stories/royal-palaces-of-abomey/) of the Dahomey kings are a day trip or overnight from Cotonou.
- **The coast.** Grand-Popo's long beach and the lagoons near the Togo border make a slower end to a trip. Mind the currents: the FCDO warns they are very strong all along the coast.

Our [10-day itinerary](/stories/benin-10-day-itinerary/) puts these together into a route that works with the advice as it stands, and our [first-timer's guide](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-benin/) covers the basics.

## Keeping Pendjari on the list

It is hard to write about a place like this and then tell you to wait. But the people best placed to tell you when the waiting is over are your own government and an operator on the ground who watches the north every week. Keep an eye on the [FCDO's Benin page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin), and if you want to talk through what a northern trip would look like when it becomes possible, a Cotonou-based operator can explain how they read the advice and what they would change.
